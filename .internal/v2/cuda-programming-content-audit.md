## Original visual treatments and exact models

1. Thread ownership/dispatch floor: 64-thread blocks, 32-lane zoom, bounds masks and synthetic two-SM/two-slot dispatch waves. It is not a hardware scheduler model.
2. Address connections: follow one lane to its 32-byte sector; aligned/offset/strided/reversed access sets. Counts are requests for the stated instruction, not measured HBM traffic.
3. Shared-memory queue rack: distinct words per bank serialize; repeated readers of one word form one broadcast request. Logical service rounds are not clock cycles.
4. GEMM residency walkthrough: exact 4×4 input/output arithmetic with k-slice staging and thread-owned accumulator regions. Full-product requested loads are 128/64/32; shared operand reads are 0/128/64. Larger register regions also enlarge the block tile. Final C stores and caches are explicitly excluded from these counters.
5. Reduction fold: all 32 input contributions sum to 528; only the active prefix owns current partial results. Full-warp participation is explicit in the reference snippet.
6. Two-buffer schedule: separate copy/compute resources with per-slot lifetime bands. No overwrite before the previous consumer finishes. Positive constant L/C and two reusable buffers support the ideal makespan equation.
7. Tile-traversal/cache experiment: row-major vs a 4×4 Hilbert path; explicitly ordered A-row then B-column accesses to an initially empty, fully associative LRU teaching cache. Capacity four yields 20 vs 12 operand loads. This is not GPU cache prediction or a promise of CUDA block order.
8. Static XOR storage diagram: 8×8 16-byte chunks in an aligned 1 KiB region; offset XOR ((offset AND 0x380) shifted right by 3). It preserves rows and low four byte bits, is bijective/self-inverse, and spreads the tracked first-word column accesses across eight banks. No claim to simulate a complete Tensor Core transaction.
