## Explanatory spine

Question: which resource limits this workload, and how does that limit change when the shape, precision, batch or device count changes?

Flow: metric/window → operations and bytes → matching hardware ceilings → actual timing/utilization → capacity/parallel ownership → committed calendar progress and cost. Keep model arithmetic, executed arithmetic and useful progress separate. A roofline is an ideal bound, not a measured latency predictor or proof of the active hardware bottleneck.

Performance owns numerical resource accounting. Practice owns experiment design and operational decisions; Parallelism owns layouts and schedules; Communication owns protocols and transport; Profiling owns measured attribution; Serving owns cache and scheduler mechanisms. Link those owners rather than duplicate their whole readers. Necessary resource equations and original worked conditions remain here.

- Roofline intensity above ridge establishes the compute ceiling in this two-resource model, not measured compute saturation. Latency, dependencies, inefficient instructions and extra traffic can dominate either side. The sum of two ideal resource times is not an upper bound on actual execution when overheads are omitted.
- BF16weight traffic cannot use an FP32arithmetic roof as a quantitative serving crossover. Match the operation's precision/instruction path. Hardwareexample precision and SKU remain scoped until exact vendor source is checked.
- Generic queue latency is not universally exponential near saturation. p99 is a percentile threshold, not the latency of every request in the worst1%. RTT is a particular end-to-end boundary, not every definition of latency.
