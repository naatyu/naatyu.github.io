### Variant-specific primary checks

Targeted reads, not complete primary audits: [GSPO equations5–10](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18071v2), [MiniMax-M1 section3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13585v1), and [CTPO sections2–3 and setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07331v1).

GSPO uses the geometric mean of likelihood ratios and a differentiated sequence surrogate; its scale and clip ranges differ from a full trajectory weight. CISPO instead uses detached clipped weights multiplying advantage and token log probability. The report disables an effective lower weight bound; a bounded weight does not remove that token's score gradient.

CTPO's exact prefix argument requires a state/action-dependent advantage. A sampled terminal outcome also depends on the suffix. Its practical group-outcome surrogate, clipping and normalization therefore must not inherit the general unbiasedness theorem. The square-root clip scaling assumes independent equal-variance log ratios; real autoregressive increments need not satisfy this. The reported tool-math comparison uses Qwen3-4B/14B, five turns,8k trajectory limit, batch512×8, LR1e−6 and avg@32. Complete proof/appendix/code and figure review remain open.

Independent two-token counterexample: terminal R=a1*a2, first-token p=.5 for both policies, target second-token p=.8 but behavior p=.2. Full-ratio first-logit gradient is .2. Prefix-only sampled-outcome gradient is .05. Using target Q(a1)=.8*a1 restores .2. This separates an exact change of measure from a practical outcome surrogate rather than rejecting the method's reported utility.

## Corrections and proof conditions

1. Bellman optimality uses max; policy evaluation uses the action distribution. γ<1 finite/bounded assumptions give the contraction. γ=1 needs other episodic/proper-policy assumptions.
2. An action-independent baseline leaves the expected policy gradient unchanged. A same-group mean includes the scored rollout itself. For iid groups its centered reward gradient is scaled by 1−1/G; RLOO uses the other G−1 rollouts.
3. The clipped surrogate is min(rA,clip(r,l,u)A), maximized. It does not force ratios inside bounds or guarantee a KL trust region. Beneficial positive updates flatten above u; beneficial negative updates flatten below l. Adverse-side derivatives can remain large.
4. Response-mean original GRPO differs from total-valid-token reduction. A fixed G×Lmax denominator is another choice. Reward std conventions and epsilon must be explicit. Scaling and reduction changes are not universal debiasing.
5. Entropy bonus has +λH in a maximized objective and −λH in a minimized loss. Entropy-controlled clip bounds are feedback heuristics, not a monotonic-entropy theorem.
6. A binary reward has entropy h₂(p)≤1 bit. This is an average-information statement. Individual surprisal −log₂p can be arbitrarily large. Token entropy≤log₂V does not bound individual token surprisal by log₂V.
7. Positive softmax support does not make success cheaply reachable. Larger batches can reduce gradient-estimator variance; a smaller step size does not increase information in an individual sample.
8. A geometric mean of token ratios is not full trajectory importance sampling. Stable log sums can remain finite when exponentiation overflows. Top-p support and recorded temperature must match the actual behavior distribution.
9. The nonnegative exp(x)−x−1 estimator equals forward KL in expectation only under the matching current conditional policy. Old-policy samples or changed prefix distributions require appropriate treatment.
10. All-equal centered rewards imply zero task advantage, not necessarily zero optimizer gradient. KL, process signals, another baseline or weight decay can remain active.
11. Finite failed attempts cannot establish zero support. Coverage tax is not a proof that RL cannot learn. Report attempts differ in harness/compute; match these conditions before causal claims.
12. PTGS uses a sampled Beta success estimate, not the posterior mean by default. Its target pivots temperature; it does not force actual success to the target. Save one assigned temperature for the entire group and all turns.
13. MiMo GAR redistributes only defined passing positive advantage mass; soft/graded cases and zero denominators need explicit fallback. Detached ratios versus differentiated clipped surrogates are not interchangeable.
14. Partial rollouts can span policy versions. Do not claim the whole trajectory came from one behavior checkpoint; keep per-segment/token provenance. Completion quotas and staleness correction solve different problems.
